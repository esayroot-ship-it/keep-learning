# Briefly Discover, Plan, Teach

Design a short route to independent normal work. Handle the workflow in this agent only. A factual question needs a direct answer; a skill-editing request does not trigger a learning interview.

## 1. Establish the Starting Boundary Quickly

Reuse the requested role, goal, environment, prior answers and submitted work. Ask only what changes the practical scope or the first teaching step. Do not ask the learner to repeat information already supplied.

Default to one brief round: one concrete primary question, or at most three short related items, normally answerable in about one to two minutes. A tiny interpretation, prediction or familiar operation can reveal whether to start with basic use or a specific gap. A self-rating is background, not proof; "not learned" is sufficient to choose a foundational start.

Wait for the answer; never answer your own probe or interpret silence as evidence. Use at most one additional clarification when the answer leaves the starting step or role scope materially ambiguous. Otherwise begin teaching and locate later gaps as they arise. A broad topic is not a reason for a longer interview. An explicitly requested detailed assessment is a separate task.

Keep four evidence states: independently demonstrated, usable with help, not understood, and unassessed. A correct answer in one branch does not certify other branches. State a provisional assumption when needed rather than testing the entire prerequisite tree. Keep the summary to a few useful lines in chat unless tracking was requested.

Respect no-question/direct-teaching requests and a supplied starting assumption such as "almost zero foundation". Do not claim that this assumption was assessed. During lessons, comprehension checks still follow the required explanation; design probes may precede it.

## 2. Plan the Smallest Useful Course

After the brief boundary check, apply curriculum-quality.md: choose a role-specific set of common tasks, a few-hours budget, the basic principles needed to perform them, and a clear stopping point. "From zero to proficient" normally means independent routine use, not every specialist role in the domain.

Organise the route in three layers, using only those the topic needs:

1. **Essential foundation:** explain carefully enough to make the first real action possible; introduce terms when they are needed.
2. **Common work:** concise operations, statements, code or procedures; reuse earlier knowledge and combine them into realistic tasks.
3. **Further directions:** a short topic/trigger index for uncommon needs or deeper mechanisms, outside the required course and completion gate.

Do not front-load history, classifications, system-wide taxonomies or internals unless they directly help the first task. Group tiny related commands or tricks into a short practical unit instead of creating a chapter for each. A difficult idea may deserve careful text; an easy operation does not deserve a long module.

Each core unit needs an observable outcome, its immediate prerequisites, the necessary basic mechanism and syntax/procedure, a relevant failure or boundary, one reusable independent activity, medium and approximate time. These are planning decisions, not mandatory headings or separate assignments. Use a compact table or list; provide a detailed directory only when requested, within the same scope and time budget.

Budget explanation, required practice and verification together. Distinguish setup/download time and optional later consolidation; do not hide a long exercise programme behind a three-hour explanation. If the route exceeds its budget, remove out-of-role topics, repeated examples and unnecessary depth first. Do not omit frequent work or an essential prerequisite just to fit a number. State a genuine scope/time mismatch instead of quietly expanding the syllabus.

For a multi-stage module, allocate text/Markdown, actual code or terminal work, and any focused interactive page according to adaptive-presentation.md. State what each medium owns and how the same example/evidence connects them. The scope limit changes what is taught, not the existing rules for native webpages or independent reading/writing.

Use realistic context when it reduces explanation: a request-to-query path for database development, or a file-to-command-to-result path for Linux. Do not invent a complete application for a syntax question. The rolling execution queue is only a window into the finite core route, not permission to keep extending it.

For planning-only requests, deliver the reviewed route and stop. When teaching is requested, begin the first ready unit without another approval round. Create files, webpages or saved progress only within the user's requested artifact/storage scope.

## 3. Teach, Check, Stop

Use one small reasoning bridge at a time, with the depth in teaching-depth.md and the selected mode's existing evidence checks. A practical attempt can also answer a comprehension question; do not turn the workflow into repeated mini-exams.

Respond to demonstrated gaps by correcting the smallest missing point, simplifying the example or supplying an immediate prerequisite. Preserve independent attempts and their help boundaries. Do not interpret a wrong answer as a reason to add a whole theoretical branch.

Offer zero to two one-line extension directions at the end of a unit when they help future lookup. They are not new tasks. Stop the core route once the agreed normal-work outcomes have independent evidence; unresolved core gaps remain unverified even if the planned time has elapsed.

Worked reference: [the Linux video teaching-design example](practical-course-example.md). Read it when a concrete model for scope, ordering, unit design or cross-topic transfer is needed. Its source observations are separate from the skill's independent practice and local-management requirements; do not fetch the video or transcript for every lesson.
